A bunch of interesting stuff just happened in the blogosphere in the past few days, so I'm going to point some of them out:

Jason Calcanis [made an "Ask Jason" post](http://calacanis.weblogsinc.com/entry/1234000050059529/) that's got some good questions related to online business and working in the [Web 2.0](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_2.0) world.

Heather Green at BW notes that AOL is planning to launch a [Podcast search utility](http://www.businessweek.com/the_thread/blogspotting/archives/2005/09/searcheable_pod.html?campaign_id=rss_blog_blogspotting), which would be one of the significant technology leaps I referred to when I said that [podcasts were gonna be big](http://www.jason-preston.com/?p=17). I hope it works, AOL.

Shortly after Google launched its blog search, [Jeff Jarvis complained](http://www.buzzmachine.com/index.php/2005/09/14/google-blog-search-of-what/) about the insane number of spam-blogs it was picking up. [Fred Wilson](http://avc.blogs.com/a_vc/2005/09/spam_blogs.html) is having the same problems. Interesting that Google is having that problem while IceRocket isn't.

Finally, my friend Jeremy pointed me to Mark Kleiman's [post on the music industry](http://www.markarkleiman.com/archives/paying_for_nonrival_goods_/2005/09/future_of_music_and_movies_and_books.php), which was an interesting read. I haven't followed the developments of music in a while, and it's good to know that I haven't missed much; execs are still trying to work in a new world with old strategies.
