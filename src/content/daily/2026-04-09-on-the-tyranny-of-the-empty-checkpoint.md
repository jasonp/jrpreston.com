---
title: "On the tyranny of the empty checkpoint"
description: "When you save your work into the void, what's actually being saved?"
pubDate: 2026-04-09
tags: [ai, workflow]
---

I keep noticing how often the AI tools I use suggest "checkpoint your progress" and how rarely the checkpoint is *for* anything. It's a defensive crouch borrowed from version control, repurposed for conversations whose state was never meant to persist.

The interesting question isn't whether to checkpoint. It's: what would the next session actually need that the conversation can't reconstruct? Most of the time, the answer is "nothing." The conversation is the checkpoint.

Where the checkpoint earns its keep is in the small set of decisions that cost you something to make and would cost you the same thing to make again. Save those. Skip the rest.
