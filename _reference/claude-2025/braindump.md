Jason Preston - Bio for TK / Seedship

Summary
Jason is 39 years old and spent the first 14 years of his career in technology events. He started in 2005 working on the Blog Business Summit, then worked on early Facebook and Twitter conferences. He worked alongside the early team at Twitter to connect Silicon Valley and Hollywood, verifying Twitter accounts and launching a number of household names on the platform, including William Shatner and Pee-Wee Herman. In 2012, Jason co-founded the Dent Conference, which is dedicated to helping people “put a dent in the universe.” The conference has been a study in entrepreneurship and success, and a source of inspiration and fuel for many notable leaders of our time, including Lyft CEO David Risher, Astronaut and Voyager Space CEO Tim Kopra, and Pixar co-founder Alvy Ray Smith. 

As a result of Jason’s work at Dent, attendees approached him with questions about leadership, company culture, and paths to success, which led him to create a consulting business to help companies like Microsoft and nonprofits like Malaria No More with strategy and culture. In 2015, he joined the board of the national medical nonprofit Hydrocephalus Association, becoming chair of the board in 2022. 

Jason joined Alsop Louie Partners as a Venture Partner in 2019 and became a Partner in 2022. He has spent the last five years learning the craft of venture capital from some of the best VCs in the business. Jason lives in Seattle, WA, with his wife and two children.



Life History

Jason was born on April 23, 1985, in Houston, Texas, and was the first of three children. His father worked at Compaq as an electrical engineer and then at Microsoft. During his time at Compaq, Jason's father contributed to the team that reverse-engineered IBM PC compatibility and broke open the home computer market.

When Jason was seven years old, his family moved to Bellevue, Washington. Growing up near Microsoft, he and his friends formed imaginary software companies, went on field trips to the Microsoft campus, and suffered from delusions of grandeur; in middle school, Jason started a business that netted him $500 making web sites for neighbors. In high school, Jason played varsity tennis, joined the advanced jazz choir, and took lead roles in several school plays.

Jason earned a bachelor's degree in diplomacy and world affairs from Occidental College in Los Angeles in 2007. While there, he started a video game blog called Flicker Gaming. Flicker didn’t succeed as a company, but it did get Jason his first job; he was for a brief time a writer for The Escapist Magazine, which provides news and commentary about video games and online culture. 

After graduating, Jason moved back to Seattle, where he met and married his wife, Mónica Guzmán, and took up a career in technology events and social media consulting. He worked for Parnassus Group, a social media agency and event producer that launched early conferences on business blogging, Facebook, LinkedIn, and Twitter. He conceived and launched the Parnassus Group’s social media events around Facebook and Twitter, which gave him the opportunity to meet and learn from the people who were building the tools and platforms driving the Web 2.0 revolution. 

Jason’s work on the Twitter Conference led to the creation of the Tweet House, a hospitality suite and event series that connected Hollywood with Silicon Valley. He hosted Tweet House at film festivals like Sundance and SXSW, featuring celebrities like LeVar Burton and Joseph Gordon-Levitt. Shortly after Steve Jobs passed away in 2011, Jason left Parnassus Group and together with Steve Broback co-founded Dent The Future, a company dedicated to helping more people “put a dent in the universe.”

Dent has built a dedicated community around a series of conferences and experiential events, beginning with the flagship annual conference first held in 2012. Over time, the Dent conference and community has attracted a diverse array of leaders from a variety of fields, from USA Today’s founding publisher Cathie Black to author and world poker champion Maria Konnikova. Each year and all year, the conference acts as a study in success: what leads to great outcomes? Why do some people succeed where others fail? A ravenous reader and student of people, Jason has spent twelve years unraveling the mysteries and bringing seers to the stage. 

In December of 2012, Jason’s newborn son, Julian, was diagnosed with hydrocephalus. It’s a condition where too much cerebrospinal fluid accumulates in the head, and it changed Jason’s life. He began volunteering with the Hydrocephalus Association,  the only national nonprofit that funds and advocates for research towards finding a cure. Jason has served nine years on HA’s board, including two as board chair. 

At HA, Jason helped create and launch the first patient-powered registry for hydrocephalus, which has led to three submitted and one published paper in medical journals. During his board tenure, HA invested $15m in targeted research grants and awards that have led to $75m in follow-on funding from the National Institutes of Health and other major funding organizations.

During this time, Jason became fascinated with what today is known as the “creator economy,” and launched a YouTube channel and streaming videogame personality called WatchSpade, whose role was to welcome newcomers and teach the skills of competitive Counter-Strike:Global Offensive. Over the two years from 2012 - 2014, WatchSpade garnered more than a million views to his videos and livestreams. In 2014, Jason’s daughter Carolina was born. 

In 2015, Jason’s wife was awarded a Nieman Fellowship at Harvard University. The family moved from Seattle, WA, to Cambridge, MA for one year and Jason was named a Nieman Associate – giving him free reign to attend classes at Harvard or MIT, which he did with wild abandon: Digital Transformation at HBS, Public Narrative at HKS, Planetary Geology at Harvard, Medical Device Innovation at MIT, and anything else he could find. 

While in Cambridge, Jason helped Harvard Business School’s Digital Initiative launch an invitation-only leadership conference, and joined the teaching staff for a workshop at the Harvard Extension School that used tabletop games to teach concepts of adaptive leadership. After the Nieman Fellowship ended in 2016, Jason moved back to Seattle with his family, where he took up a part-time lecturer position at the University of Washington, teaching Organizational Teamwork and Leadership. 

As a result of Jason’s work at Dent and UW, attendees approached him with questions about leadership, company culture, and success, which led him to found a consulting business called Capture Culture that specialized in helping companies like Microsoft and nonprofits like Malaria No More with strategy and culture.

Jason came to know Stewart Alsop as a member of the Dent community. The two of them realized that there was a lot of alignment between the work of Capture Culture and the work of helping portfolio companies at Alsop Louie succeed. This led to Jason first advising companies within the Alsop Louie portfolio and ultimately identifying early-stage companies to invest in. In 2019, Jason formally joined Alsop Louie as a Venture Partner, and in 2022 he became a Partner. 

Jason has taken board seats with two of the Alsop Louie portfolio companies where he has negotiated and led funding rounds, managed complicated board dynamics, and worked hand-in-hand with company executives through critical company moments, and assists as a board observer on a third portfolio company. The experience has been an education unlike any other, and the lessons inform Jason’s view on investing that he takes with him to TK Ventures.
