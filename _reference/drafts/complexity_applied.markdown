# Applications of complexity

I didn't know much about the Santa Fe Institute until about a month ago, when I read Michael Waldrop's 1992 book on the science of complexity (it's called [Complexity][1]). 

The insitute was born in the mid to late 1980's as a group of scientists set out to create a home for truly interdisciplinary research: no siloed departments -- instead, it would bring the theories of economics, physics, computing, and others together in a silent crash. The theory was that these academic schools of thought may in fact have something to teach each other. 

Complexity chronicles the beginnings of the Santa Fe Institute, and also the curve it took as over the first decade of its existence the reserachers gradually began to understand that the fields they had gathered all seemed to collide over complex adaptive systems. And so it was decided that the Santa Fe Institute was in the business of understanding complexity. 

This reminds me a lot of the founding philosophy of Dent. My co-founder Steve and I have long believed that the most interesting innovations come form work at the boundaries between two or more interesting disciplines. 

Much of what's in the book is a few decades old by now, and there are some understandings of complexity that are more modern. But in many ways, the science is still not well understood -- networks, borders between phase transitions, the game of life, von neumman pixel games, and more --
